The channels commute.
Their hidden geometry
still costs queries.

Two devices hide their measurement bases. Their outcomes stay private. A quantum switch can test the relation between the bases with eight device calls. The manuscript proves an exponential lower bound for ordinary access, even with quantum memory.

The main contribution

The switch test was known. The new manuscript result is the cost of the ordinary competitor for this exact promise: more than 2n/16/100 total calls for success of at least 2/3 on every valid pair. The target has n qubits.

01

Ask for one relation, not the basis.

A dephasing channel measures in a basis, then discards the outcome. The device returns the quantum system. It does not return the outcome label or the basis description.

Promise A · SAME

The devices use the same basis.

A second measurement keeps the classical information that survived the first measurement. The two devices can have different names. Their channel maps are equal.

A = B = Δℬ
A ∘ B = B ∘ A = Δℬ

Promise B · MUB

The bases are mutually unbiased.

Each vector of one basis has equal measurement probability in the other basis. Either sequence of the two devices returns the maximally mixed state.

|〈bj|ck〉|² = 1/d
A ∘ B = B ∘ A = Ω

What does a dephaser do?

Δℬ(ρ) = Σj PjρPj, where Pj = |bj〉〈bj|.

The operation removes coherence between different basis vectors. It keeps diagonal probabilities. The channel Ω sends an input X to I Tr(X)/d. For a state ρ, this output is I/d.

Source: manuscript, promise and Theorem 1; labels sec:model and thm:c6.

02

Give the ordinary learner a strong interface.

The lower bound permits arbitrary finite quantum memory. It also permits classical adaptation, early stopping, and a joint final measurement. Each call selects A or B using classical information. The devices are memoryless: a previous call does not change their channel maps.

Keep the device fixed. A new call uses the same hidden channel. The proof does not replace it with a new independent random basis. Inverse access in the proof is an analytical tool. The learner does not receive U or U†.

Source: ordinary-query model. The model is quantum circuits with classical control of query order (QC-CC), as defined in Wechs et al.

03

Make the two orders interfere.

A quantum switch uses a control qubit to connect the two orders coherently. It makes one call to A and one call to B. A classical random choice of order cannot retain the same interference term.

The simple input

Prepare the control in |+〉. Prepare the target in I/d. Run the switch. Measure the control in the X basis, {|+〉, |−〉}. The target can then be discarded.

This input is important. It makes the statistic a property of the device relation, without a known vector from either hidden basis.

The quantity that survives

The visibility v is the control’s Pauli-X expectation. For dephasers, it is the average fourth power of the basis overlap.

v = (1/d) Σj,k |〈bj|ck〉|⁴SAME: v = 1. MUB: v = 1/d. Minus probability: p− = (1 − v)/2.

Do not replace the switch with a mixture. A mixture selects an order and loses coherence between the branches. The switch retains the off-diagonal control terms. These terms reveal the projector relation even when the complete channel maps commute.

Source formulas and attribution

For general component operators Ai and Bj, the switch has Kij = |0〉〈0| ⊗ BjAi + |1〉〈1| ⊗ AiBj. The visibility is (1/d) Re Σi,j Tr(BjAiBj†Ai†).

Sources: switch definition; visibility and the four-use test, labels eq:switch, eq:visibility, eq:basis-visibility; Gao et al., 2208.06210v4.

05

Hide one common orientation. Control what leaks.

The lower bound must handle the whole adaptive learner. It cannot rely on a restricted measurement rule or a small memory. The proof compares the learner’s final quantum states under two carefully matched random inputs.

First, make a valid private cache.

The proof uses a recording theorem for one shared Haar unitary. It then converts that private record into a label cache. Each approximation has an explicit error cost.

The cache has two private branches. Retrieval uses a stored label. Birth adds a new uniform label. Together, their trace effects sum to the identity. Thus the complete cache preserves probability.

Next, use a common reference.

Replace the retrieved label’s dephaser by complete depolarization. This reference does not depend on the basis choice. Its private labels remain invariant under a common unitary rotation.

The invariant-label lemma bounds the information from one retrieval. The bound includes arbitrary entangled side information. Summing these small changes bounds the learner’s final distinguishing power.

The key information bound

Let X be a positive state of trace s on k labels and arbitrary side information Z. Suppose a common unitary rotation of all labels leaves X unchanged. Then dephasing one label differs little from depolarizing it:

‖(Δ ⊗ id)(X) − (Ω ⊗ id)(X)‖₁ ≤ 6(k − 1)s/d.

The same rotation acts on every label. Invariance of one marginal alone would not be sufficient. The proof keeps Z and its correlations throughout.

For each cache, the accumulated half-trace distance from the reference is at most 3q(q − 1)/d. The two caches differ by at most 6q(q − 1)/d. Recording and cache-construction errors must also be added.

Source: invariant-label lemma and telescoping argument, label lem:invariant-label. The conversion to a physical cache is in the main proof and Appendix A.

The difficult step is the bridge to the cache. The proof retains private histories until their weights can be made uniform. It does not erase those histories early, assume fresh outputs at every call, or condition on a successful event.

Source: shared-basis comparison and decision reduction, labels lem:c6-comparison and eq:c6-comparison. The imported recording result is Ma–Huang, Theorem 8, Eq. (9.5).

06

Three outputs require three different theorems.

A useful decision bit can be easier to obtain than a complete simulated device. Exact simulation is a further demand. Keep the task, error metric, and access contract beside each result.

Within the stated theorem model

One bit, with success at least 2/3

More than d¹⁄¹⁶/100 total ordinary calls are necessary.

The input is a fixed pair of hidden-outcome rank-one dephasers. Every pair satisfies SAME or MUB. Four switches use eight box calls and achieve one-sided error below 1/3.

Quantum memory, classical adaptation, and a collective final measurement are allowed.

Why approximation and exactness use different arguments

Approximation: hidden-label collisions

Use fixed channels that mix independent Haar unitaries. Before a private label repeats, ordinary causal order limits reverse-composition coherence. Collision and nonorthogonality errors give terms of order q²/r and r²/d. Their balance gives the fourth-root scale.

Finite approximation is possible. The manuscript gives an upper bound of 2⌈2d(d − 1)/ε⌉ total program queries for 0 < ε < 1. The lower and upper exponents are not claimed optimal.

Exactness: a finite support obstruction

Assume a finite exact universal simulator exists. Polarization reduces its identity to a diluted switch with one unknown call per channel and a positive switch coefficient. A qubit support certificate rules this out. A qubit embedding extends the result to d ≥ 2.

The certificate is exact arithmetic. It supports this reduction. It is not a numerical search that proves all possible claims by itself.

Other useful connections, with their limits

Noise and switch correlations. Ebler, Salek, and Chiribella show that useful target/control correlations can survive a switch of noisy channels. This explains why channel noise does not remove every switch effect. It does not prove the present ordinary-query lower bound.

OTOC interpretation. The manuscript identifies the visibility with a phase-averaged out-of-time-ordered correlator of rotations diagonal in the two hidden bases. This is a consequence of an exact identity and the SAME/MUB comparison. It is not another independent lower-bound proof. It specifies no time evolution or local probes. It does not certify a unitary design, dynamical scrambling, or cryptographic security.

Different OTOC access. Cotler, Schuster, and Mohseni compare forward-only and forward/inverse unitary access under an external-memory restriction. Here the learner has arbitrary quantum memory but only irreversible channel access. Roberts and Yoshida study complete probe averages and ensemble moments, which have a different scope.

08

Explain the value. Keep the boundary visible.

Start with the device relation and the access contract. Then state the gain. The lower-bound method is the added technical content.

A one-minute explanation

“Take two fixed devices that measure in hidden bases and discard their outcomes. The bases are either the same or mutually unbiased. The complete channel maps commute in both cases.

“A known quantum-switch experiment can see the difference. Four uses give a one-sided decision with error below one third. That costs eight underlying device calls.

“The new result proves that ordinary access needs more than 2n/16/100 calls, even with arbitrary finite quantum memory and classical adaptation. The proof turns one shared hidden-unitary record into a valid private cache, then limits the information that retrieval can reveal.

“Separate results address full-switch simulation. Finite-error simulation has a fourth-root dimension lower bound. Exact universal simulation has no finite worst-case ordinary budget. Those are stronger output tasks with separate proofs.”

State the claims as manuscript results. The supplied verification record has a defined scope. This guide is an explanation; it is not a new proof audit.
Strong selling points
  • A precise ordinary competitor. Memory, classical adaptation, early stopping, and joint measurement are included.
  • Commuting channel maps. The advantage does not require noncommuting complete maps.
  • An exact promise. One shared U makes every sampled pair SAME or MUB.
  • A physical proof bridge. The cache preserves trace and keeps private histories until the comparison permits their removal.
  • One coherent story, distinct outputs. Testing, approximate simulation, and exact simulation have explicit boundaries.
Limits to retain
  • The exponent 1/16 need not be optimal. The lower bound has a small constant.
  • No practical runtime advantage is measured here. Small-n numeric thresholds can be weak.
  • Visible outcome records, hidden-unitary access, or coherent unknown-box choice change the model.
  • The Haar hard prior does not give an efficient device-construction claim.
  • Keyed devices require forward-and-inverse pseudorandomness assumptions. Their consequence is computational hardness for efficient learners.

The gain is a proved limit for a clear interface.

Conditional keyed devices: what transfers?

Assume an efficient keyed unitary family Uk is pseudorandom against efficient algorithms with both forward and inverse access. Build (ΔUₖ, ΔUₖ) or (ΔUₖ, ΔUₖH). Every key has the exact promise. Four switches still work for each key.

An ordinary q-call learner can be represented using 2q forward/inverse unitary queries. Two security hybrids then transfer a bound on efficient distinguishing advantage. Forward-only pseudorandomness is insufficient for this reduction.

This gives a conditional computational statement for efficient learners. It does not give the Haar theorem’s information-theoretic exponential claim for a finite keyed family.

Source: conditional keyed implementation, label eq:pru.

10

Keep these terms at hand.

Technical terms are necessary for the mathematics. These short definitions give their meaning in this guide.

Channel / CPTP map
A valid quantum input–output operation. CPTP means completely positive and trace preserving.
Dephasing
Measurement in a basis with the outcome removed. Coherence between distinct basis vectors is lost.
Mutually unbiased bases (MUB)
Two bases with squared overlap 1/d for every pair of basis vectors.
Quantum switch
An operation that places the two device orders under coherent control.
Kraus operator
One component operator in a representation of a quantum channel. Its label is private here.
Visibility v
The X expectation of the switch control for the specified input. It sets the plus/minus probabilities.
Haar unitary
A unitary sampled from the uniform rotation-invariant probability law. It is sampled once in the hard prior.
Trace distance TD
Half the trace norm of the state difference. It determines optimal equal-prior state discrimination.
Half-diamond error
A worst-case channel difference, divided by two. It includes inputs entangled with a reference.
Ω(f)
A necessary lower growth scale, with a positive constant suppressed. It is not an exact optimum.
Worst-case query budget
The maximum number of device calls on any branch of the procedure.
Invariant private labels
A private record that stays unchanged under a common rotation of all labels, including its side-information correlations.
