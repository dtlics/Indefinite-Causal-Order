# Primary sources and attribution

**Checked:** 17 November 2026 (America/New_York).

**Scope:** the application-screen and QEC-deepening research packages. This is a bounded reference audit, not an exhaustive novelty review. The `S` identifiers below belong to the application-screen report; the `R` identifiers belong to the deepening report.

## Attribution rules for this research

The cited literature supplies the switch/NCOM definition, established QEC and recovery methods, competing characterization methods, magic-state distillation, and related examples. The packages' explicit ordinary compilers, code-recovery simulator, scalar projectors, graph counting result, finite candidate calculations, and numerical comparisons are **direct derivations or calculations in these packages**. Do not attribute these statements wholesale to a cited paper or treat the lack of a matching source as a publication-priority result.

Three attribution distinctions deserve explicit wording:

1. **Recorded ABA:** Gao et al. discuss the projective-measurement ABA experiment in Appendix D. The extension to one readable Lüders-projective arm and an arbitrary other channel follows by the displayed Kraus calculation in the report; it should be labeled an extension/deduction rather than quoted as a separate literature theorem.
2. **Unitary/dilation access:** Bavaresco et al. Theorem 1 proves repeated-unitary full-switch simulation, including additional bipartite ports. Appendix B3/B4 explains why access to a channel's dilation is stronger than access to its reduced CPTP map. The report's known-controlled-arm circuit is a direct construction under that stronger explicit access promise.
3. **Quantum programs:** Wilde et al. define environment-parametrized and environment-seizable channels. They are background for the program-state interpretation. The report's full-switch compiler is a separate derivation that additionally requires a known coherent implementation of the common processor and an obtainable program state.

## Barnum–Knill application: precise statement

Let N include the chosen encoding and the **entire** fixed noisy layout, with all output registers the decoder is allowed to retain. For one logical input state rho = I/k, define the transpose recovery with generalized inverses on the support. Theorem 3 gives

$$
F_e(\rho,R_{\mathrm{Petz}}\circ N)
\ge \left[\sup_{R\;\mathrm{CPTP}}F_e(\rho,R\circ N)\right]^2.
$$

Hence $F_{\mathrm{opt}}\le\sqrt{F_{\mathrm{Petz}}}$. This applies to the full control-target output, or to a recorded-flag output if that is the specified layout. It must **not** be applied to a normalized, input-dependent postselected branch as though that branch were a CPTP channel. A Petz bound for a measured-control channel also cannot upper-bound a decoder that retains additional control coherence unless their equivalence is proved (as it is for an identical-channel self-switch).

The report uses a single logical maximally mixed state, so there is no ensemble-commutativity obstacle. The source's 2026 correction repairs a proof issue for larger ensembles; it does not invalidate this single-state use. The six eliminations still depend on floating-point channel evaluations, and should remain identified as numerically supported inequalities with an analytical outer bound.

## Limits of the source check

The source check is bounded to these 19 unique references. S12 is abstract-level
related-work context because its full text could not be retrieved; no project
theorem or numerical conclusion depends on it. Bibliographic verification does
not establish priority for the direct constructions. None of these references
turns a fixed-layout numerical gain into a substantial unrestricted
ordinary-query advantage for the practitioner tasks in this branch.
